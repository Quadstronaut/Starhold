### 🔑 One deliberate "expensive" choice in the lean build

I kept the **$109.99 Gigabyte 4-DIMM board** instead of an $89.99 2-DIMM ASRock. **This is the most important $20 in the lean build.** Its only real weakness is 16 GB of RAM, and a 4-slot board means he adds a second 16 GB kit later instead of throwing the first one away.

⚠️ **Caveat on that upgrade:** AM5 runs memory noticeably slower with all four DIMM slots populated. The *clean* upgrade is to sell/keep the 16 GB kit and drop in a single 2×16 GB kit. Budget for that, not for "just add sticks."

### 📐 How those CPU scores were derived

All figures are Tom's Hardware **1080p Gaming Score**, expressed on the current hierarchy's scale.

- **Read directly** from the [2026 CPU hierarchy](https://www.tomshardware.com/reviews/cpu-hierarchy,4312.html): Ryzen 5 7600X3D **80.6%**, Ryzen 7 7700X **70.6%**, Ryzen 5 7600X **67.3%**, Core i5-14400 **58.0%**. *(The i5-14400F is the same silicon as the i5-14400 minus the iGPU — identical CPU performance.)*
- **ᵃ Bridged** from Tom's [legacy hierarchy page](https://www.tomshardware.com/reviews/cpu-hierarchy,4312-2.html), which ranks the non-X chips but on a different baseline (i9-13900K = 100%). The **Ryzen 5 7600X and Ryzen 7 7700X appear on both pages**, giving two independent conversion anchors that agree to within **0.7%** — so the bridge is sound. Legacy figures: 7700X 87.13%, R7 7700 86.19%, 7600X 83.62%, R5 7600 79.74%, R7 5700X 69.50%. Multiplying by 67.3 ÷ 83.62 = **0.805** converts to the current scale. Validation: 7700X converts to 70.1% against its actual 70.6% — a 0.5-point error.
- **ᵇ Inferred, not measured.** The i5-13400F is not ranked by Tom's. It is the same Raptor Lake silicon as the i5-14400, same LGA1700, same 2.50 GHz base, with the 14400 boosting 100 MHz higher (4.70 vs 4.60 GHz). So it sits a point or two under 58%. I'm labelling this as inference.
- **ᶜ Upper bound.** The **Ryzen 7 5700 is not the 5700X.** It's a Cezanne part with **16 MB of L3 cache — half the 5700X's 32 MB** ([Tom's Hardware](https://www.tomshardware.com/news/cezanne-lives-on-ryzen-7-5700-surfaces-without-integrated-graphics)). The 5700X converts to **55.9%**, and the 5700 sits below that. I'm not quoting a precise figure because Tom's doesn't rank it and the halved cache makes extrapolation unreliable.

> ⚠️ One trap I avoided: Tom's legacy page carries a **second** table (i9-12900K = 100%) listing the Ryzen 7 5700G at 76.61%. It shares **no CPUs** with the table above it, so that number cannot be compared across scales. It is excluded.

### 🔴 Where Skytech loses — and why it matters for *this* library

1. **Every model in his price range ships 16 GB except one.** That is precisely the spec Rust breaks on. The one 32 GB option (SHADOW 4 #2064-B) pairs it with a **Ryzen 7 5700** — an old Zen 3 chip on dead-end AM4 — and an 8 GB GPU.

2. **The CPUs are wrong for WoW — and now the gap is measured, not asserted.** Our 7600X3D scores **80.6%** on Tom's 1080p gaming index. **Every Skytech model in this band is 16% to 44%+ behind it** (table above). None have 3D V-Cache, the single biggest lever in WoW raids and Rust.

   The cheapest model, the ARCHANGEL 4, has the **second-weakest CPU of the whole group** at ~57% — roughly **41% behind** our chip. Its better GPU doesn't help in WoW, because WoW raid frames are CPU-bound.

3. **🔴 The PSU is unbranded.** Skytech's own spec sheets say only **"650W GOLD"** and **"850W GOLD ATX 3.0"** — no manufacturer, no model. This is the component most likely to take the rest of the machine with it. Ours is a named **MSI MAG A750GL** with a public review record.

   This isn't theoretical. r/buildapc, *"New Skytech Prebuilt Nightmare"* — an Azure 3 owner with random shutdowns and display dropouts mid-driver-install. Top replies: *"Could be a shitty dud PSU or something"* and *"Sounds like some kind of hardware failure. Could be a bad PSU."* Other threads include *"Got Wrong GPU in a Prebuilt from Skytech."*

4. **Warranty is 1 year, parts and labor.** Buying separately, he'd hold **10 years on the PSU, 5 on the SSD, 3 on the GPU, lifetime on the RAM** — each RMA'd directly with the maker.

5. **Dead-end platform on the Intel models.** LGA1700 (13400F/14400F) is finished. AM5 is supported into 2027+, so a CPU upgrade later is a drop-in.

## 5. What Changed Since The First Draft

- **Storage was revised.** My original pick, the Silicon Power UD90, was **rejected after the Reddit pass** — *"No. It's QLC"*, *"it was stealth changed to QLC"*, *"it failed me again and again."* QLC cache exhaustion is exactly what Rust's asset streaming exposes. Replaced with the TLC **WD Blue SN5100**.
- **Lean build simplified.** It now shares the board, PSU, SSD and monitor with the optimal build — fewer SKUs, and the shared parts are the ones I verified hardest.
- **Lean SSD upgraded** from the Patriot P400 Lite to the SN5100 rather than risk a second QLC trap.
- **Lean price rose** from $1,165.61 to $1,227.62 as a result. I think that's the right call; the alternatives were a 2-DIMM board that blocks the RAM upgrade and an unverified budget SSD.
- **PSU note carried forward:** owners report premature fan failure on early **A750GL** units; the newer **A750GLS** revision uses a different 135 mm fan. If the GLS shows up at a similar price, take it.
