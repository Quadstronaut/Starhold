04

Where It Falls Short

The parts of this you should know before you spend anything. None of it is a dealbreaker, but you shouldn't find out later.

It's over the original budget, and it's the memory's fault

The target was around $1,500 all-in. Memory and storage prices have gone insane this year — data centres buying everything for AI. That 32 GB kit is $379.99; a year ago it was about $90. Same part, four times the price. Nothing clever gets around it, and waiting probably makes it worse, not better.

1 TB of storage, not 2 TB

Same reason. It's enough for Windows and a healthy stack of games, but Rust and modern titles are big. Doubling it is +$97.95 whenever you want, and it's the first upgrade worth making.

GTA VI readiness is a bet

The game isn't out and nobody has benchmarks. The 16 GB graphics card is the informed hedge — it's what you'd want if it turns out demanding. It is not a promise, and anyone telling you otherwise is guessing too.

Two separate orders

Cheapest prices are split across Newegg and Amazon, so it's two carts and two lots of shipping. Buying everything from one shop is simpler but costs more.

Windows isn't in the price

You can install Windows and run it unactivated — free, legal, works fully. You get a small watermark in the corner and can't change some personalisation settings. Most people run like that for years. A real licence is about $139 whenever you feel like it.

Now the catch. Look at the gaming score column. Every one of those machines is between 16% and 44% behind on the processor — and WoW, the game you'll play most, depends on the processor rather than the graphics card. A faster graphics card can't fix that.

Two more things worth noticing:

The AZURE 3 costs $1,649.99 — more than this entire build — while being slower and shipping half the memory. It only ever looks competitive with a coupon stapled to it.

And every one of them lists its power supply as just "650W GOLD". No brand. No model. That's the one component that can kill everything else when it fails, and you're not told what it is. Ours is a named MSI unit you can go read reviews of. Their warranty is one year on everything; buying the parts yourself you'd hold 10 years on the power supply, 5 on the drive, 3 on the graphics card, each claimed directly with the manufacturer.

Straight answer: if you mostly play WoW and Rust, build this one. If you'd rather have something that shows up in a box and you genuinely don't want a project, the ARCHANGEL 4 is a fair buy — just know WoW will run worse and you'll want more memory within a year.
